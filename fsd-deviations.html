QA reference

Where the build differs from the spec, and why

A comparison of two things only: the input — the Confluence FSD and the Figma frames — against the output, what actually ships. 78 differences across 12 sections, plus 22 findings worth knowing before you test.

Everything listed here is a decision. If you find something that differs from the spec and is not on this page, that is worth raising.

01 — How to read this

What is here, and what is not

Every entry is written as three lines: what the spec and design say, what ships, and why they differ. You can check any of them against the Confluence page, the Figma frame and the built section — nothing here needs the development history to make sense.

DiffersThe build does something other than what the spec or the frame says. Read the reason before raising it.
Not builtThe spec asks for something the build deliberately does not do. Read these first — they are the ones most likely to be logged as bugs.
AddedThe build does something the spec never asked for, usually because accessibility or the CMS vocabulary required it.
FindingNot a spec-versus-build difference, but worth knowing: something blocked on TVS, unverified, or a risk the design does not cover.

Tablet widths are best-effort, and are not part of this review

Figma tokenises its desktop frames at 1200 and above and its mobile frames at 768 and below, so the band between them is not drawn in any design we were given. Every value a section uses in that band — where it changes column count, how many cards sit in a row, when the drawn type sizes step in — was chosen to fit the screen sensibly rather than measured from a frame. Those choices are not listed per section and are not defects. The drawn widths, 1512 and 375, are the ones to review; the captures on each section's reference page are taken at both.

Two kinds of note are deliberately left out. Of the 152 deviation notes recorded across the twelve specs, 13 describe how the build got somewhere rather than where it ended up — a value corrected against Figma, a helper extracted, a first attempt replaced — and 11 record behaviour in the tablet band, which the note above covers once instead of section by section. Both remain in each section's _fsd.md. A build-time check asserts that every one of the 152 is either published above or explicitly classified, so nothing reaches this page by accident and nothing falls off it either.

03 — Applies to every module

Stated once, true throughout

These are the same in most or all twelve sections. Each section below lists which of them apply to it rather than repeating the reasoning.

Impression tracking is not built

Not built

Spec & design
Every FSD's Other requirements asks for automated impression tracking on viewport entry.
What ships
Only click tracking ships, through the shared raiseEvent('ItemClick', …) handler.
Why
No impression event name appears anywhere in the ten downloaded TVS templates — only ItemClick and the add-to-cart event. Building one means inventing a name TVS's handler would ignore. Blocked on TVS supplying the event name and payload.

Per-module structured data is replaced by one page-level emitter

Not built

Spec & design
The Global SEO FSD asks each high-value module (Product, Review, FAQ, Video) to emit its own structured data, with a switch per module to turn it on or off.
What ships
The global-seo section is the single page-level emission point — BreadcrumbList, a summary ItemList of featured products, and VideoObject. No module emits its own, and there are no per-module toggles.
Why
Google retired FAQ and HowTo rich results in 2023, requires single-product pages for Product rich results, and treats markup describing content not visible on the page as a manual-action trigger. Per-module emission with merchandiser toggles multiplies invalid states while earning nothing.

Interaction and async states are mostly ours to fill in

Added

Spec & design
The design system specifies states where a component owns them — the button's hover and disabled are drawn at Figma 7078:62, and the icon button carries a State property. Section frames place their instances at State=Default, which is the normal way to compose a layout, so per-section hover, focus and pressed treatments are not drawn. Loading, error and out-of-stock are outside what a static frame expresses at all.
What ships
Where the design system specifies a state we follow it exactly. Everything else is built: a visible focus ring on every control, disabled carousel arrows at the ends of a track, skeleton cards while a product request is in flight, a disabled Add to Cart reading “Out of Stock”, and polite live regions announcing async results.
Why
Accessibility is a delivery requirement rather than a spec line, so these are built rather than deferred until they are specified. They are the states QA will see with no frame to compare against — anything that looks wrong here is a design pass waiting to happen rather than a defect.

Heading level, size and typeface are merchandiser fields

Added

Spec & design
Most FSDs say only “Admin can edit the heading”.
What ships
Every section with a heading also carries headingLevel, headingSize and headingFont.
Why
Heading level keeps the page's heading order unbroken when modules stack, and it changes nothing visual. The defaults reproduce the drawn design, so a merchandiser who touches nothing gets the frame.

Every section offers an optional Section ID

Added

Spec & design
Only the Product Carousel FSD asks for an anchor target, and marks it Required.
What ships
Every section carries an optional sectionId, constrained to lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens.
Why
It is how the Sticky Anchor Navigation targets a module and how page-level CSS reaches one placement. Optional everywhere rather than required in one form, so the same field does not mean two things.

Headings drawn in Fields render in a fallback serif

Differs

Spec & design
Benefits Highlight, Testimonials, Shop By Goal, Product Grid, Static Bundle, Shoppable UGC and Product Carousel all draw their heading in Fields.
What ships
Fields is offered and is the default in every one of those except Product Carousel, which defaults to Lato. Fields is not @font-face-declared on vitaminshoppe.com, so it resolves to Georgia.
Why
Checked against the live site: customFonts.css and tvs-header.css self-host Lato, the Corisande family, louisiana and thehistoriasolid, and nothing named Fields appears in any @font-face rule. Georgia keeps the module reading as a serif rather than silently becoming Lato. Every content item is already correct the moment TVS supply the file. The two product modules are inconsistent with each other until this is answered.

The click-tracking payload is unverified against a real landing page

Finding

Spec & design
Click tracking “via the standard raiseEvent handler”.
What ships
raiseEvent('ItemClick', item, '<Section Name>'), sending { ctaLabel, destinationUrl, moduleTitle }, guarded by typeof so a page without the handler cannot swallow a navigation.
Why
raiseEvent is not in the TVS-Common reference. The shape is copied from TVS's own components, and the payload keys are our guess at what their handler reads. Nothing has been confirmed against a live LP.

04 — The shared product card

Product Carousel and Product Grid

Both product modules render the same card, so they share its differences. Static Bundle and Shoppable UGC draw their own product rows and are covered in their own sections.

Products resolve through getProductDetailsBulk, not getProductList

Differs

Spec & design
The FSDs point at a TVS API guideline, in which getProductList is the endpoint intended for cards and tiles.
What ships
One getProductDetailsBulk call, shared by every product module on the page.
Why
getProductList returns no compare-at price, no badge fields and no skuId — its add-to-cart identifier is jdaSkId, which means the JDA id on a different endpoint. The spec requires a compare-at price and the design requires a badge, so the lighter call cannot render the card. Two product modules on one page are still a single request.

The struck compare-at price renders only on a genuine sale

Differs

Spec & design
“Price (compare at + sale price)”.
What ships
The strikethrough requires both the onSale flag and a list price genuinely higher than the current one.
Why
The API returns products whose before and after prices are identical with onSale false. Comparing values alone would advertise a discount of zero as a discount.

The offer line is hidden and its detail moved into the badge glyph

Differs

Spec & design
Both the design and the live TVS tile put promoMessage as a text line under the review count.
What ships
That line does not render. Any offer copy that is not an auto-delivery message — “Buy One, Get One 50% Off”, “Ships Free!”, “Only at the Vitamin Shoppe” — is joined to the tooltipMessage legal detail and shown in a panel behind the info glyph, which moves into the badge.
Why
Hidden at the client's request: 36 of the 45 SKUs on the creatine collection carry nothing but “Auto-Delivery Save 15%”, and auto-delivery is not being merchandised in these modules. A SKU with offer copy and no badge renders the glyph alone, so its position never moves.

The product name hugs its content instead of reserving three lines

Differs

Spec & design
The desktop frame draws the name as a fixed three-line box — “three lines at 72” — and reserving that height is what lines the rating, price and button rows up across a row of cards.
What ships
The name hugs whatever it holds, up to a three-line clamp. The rating row therefore sits wherever each name leaves it, so a two-line name puts its stars a line lower than its neighbours. Prices and buttons still line up, because they are pushed to the bottom of the card.
Why
Removed on the client's instruction 2026-08-11: real product names run to one or two lines on most SKUs, so the reserved third line was dead space under every short name. One knock-on worth expecting — the loading skeleton's name placeholder is a nominal three lines rather than an exact box, so a card shortens slightly when a short name lands.

The whole card is one link, and the brand line is no longer one

Differs

Spec & design
The design draws no link affordances at all, so which elements are anchors was ours to decide.
What ships
The product name carries an ::after inset over the whole card, so the card is a single hit area and a single tab stop. The image anchor is gone and the brand line is a <span>. Add-to-cart and the info glyph sit above the overlay rather than inside the anchor.
Why
It was four anchors per card, which put five tab stops on every card and 40 on a full carousel. This drops the brand page as a destination — a deliberate trade for the tab order.

A dead SKU is dropped, and a module with none removes itself

Differs

Spec & design
Neither the FSD nor the design says what happens to a SKU that no longer resolves.
What ships
That card is dropped. If every SKU fails, or the product API is unreachable, the whole section removes itself rather than leaving a heading over an empty row. The failure is logged to the console.
Why
Implied by the spec but never stated. Static Bundle deliberately behaves differently — see its own entry.

Which rating field renders is unconfirmed

Finding

Spec & design
“Rating — admin can decide to display the rating or not for each section.”
What ships
avgDecimalRating is what renders.
Why
docs/tvs-api-reference.md records that avgOverallRating and avgDecimalRating are labelled “actual” and “rounded”, and that the sample data contradicts those labels in both directions. Needs checking against live data before launch.

Whether TVS's mini-cart opens on add is unconfirmed

Finding

Spec & design
Add to Cart “triggers add to cart action and open a success message 'added to cart' (current live site behavior)”.
What ships
The documented promise API, cart.addToCart, with confirmation shown on the button itself.
Why
Whether TVS's global mini-cart also opens on that path, or only on the legacy raiseEvent path, has not been confirmed. If the mini-cart is expected, this is the item to test first.

SEO title and meta description are not fields on this section

Differs

Spec & design
“All pages must include dedicated, editable fields for SEO Title and Meta Description tags.”
What ships
Neither field appears on this content type.
Why
cs-landing-page.seoTags already owns both and we already push them per page. A second source of truth would drift from the first. The fields exist; they are just not here.

The module renders nothing at all

Differs

Spec & design
“Content crawlability: modules must not be hidden; they must render as real HTML text and be exposed to crawlers.”
What ships
This section renders no content and carries aria-hidden="true" on an empty root. Its entire output is head metadata.
Why
The crawlability clause governs content modules — copy must be real text rather than an image. Head metadata is real markup in the served document, and nothing readable is hidden from anyone. Worth knowing before it is raised as an empty module.

Differs

Spec & design

What ships
The section sets prerenderReady false at injection and true once its payload is emitted.
Why
The FSD predates the blank-snapshot captures we observed. This turns the snapshot moment from a network-idle guess into a signal, which is more than the spec asks for.

06 — slim-hero-banner-a

slim-hero-banner-a

Template 1Template 2

reference page and design comparison

Differences 7

The eyebrow is built to the spec, not to the frames

Differs

Spec & design
The Admin requirement gives an optional eyebrow at a 24-character limit. Neither current Figma frame draws one — it was present only in the earlier 15755:2502 frame.
What ships
Built: optional, maxLength: 24, uppercase, brand blue, at 16px desktop / 14px mobile with 0.08em tracking.
Why
The spec is the contract, so the field exists. The type scale is ours — a step under the smallest headline pair so it reads as a label. It needs a design call if TVS have a specific treatment in mind.

The heading font list resolves the spec's “List TBD”

Differs

Spec & design
“Admin can change the font family by selecting alternate font from dropdown. (List TBD)”
What ships
Lato and corisanderegular, later joined by Fields.
Why
Resolved by observation rather than assumption: those were the only two text typefaces @font-face-declared and in use on the live site. Chirag's 2026-07-29 email lists four values, but corisandelight and corisandebold are weight variants of the same family rather than separate choices. Corisande ships one weight per family name, so a bold heading resolves to corisandebold.

Heading size is a merchandiser choice of six fixed pairs

Differs

Spec & design
The FSD fixes no size. Sebastian Georgescu's open comment asks whether an admin can change it, since the promo version (BOGO 50) uses a larger font than the non-promo one.
What ships
Six fixed desktop/mobile pairs. Four are the richtext-md tokens from Figma 9905:43543 — 48/30 default, 36/24, 28/20, 20/16 — and 64/38 and 56/34 are extrapolated above h1.
Why
Both promo and non-promo versions are executable. Pairs rather than two free selectors, so the breakpoints cannot be set incoherently. Line-height stays 1.2 and letter-spacing 0 at every size.

The Light button gains an edge the FSD does not mention

Differs

Spec & design
“Light: white with #0458AD text.” Nothing else. Figma's own Light equivalent fills #F2F2F2.
What ships
Both FSD values kept verbatim, plus a 2px border in the button's own text colour.
Why
This section puts the CTA inside a white card, where a white fill has a 1.00:1 boundary against its background. WCAG 1.4.11 asks for 3:1, and rendered it stops reading as a button at all. The border introduces no new colour and lifts the boundary to 6.99:1. On a dark surface it is barely perceptible.

Button colours can be overridden at page level

Differs

Spec & design
Not in the source FSD. Our earlier transcription carried a sentence the Confluence page does not — worth confirming with Nigel — so do not cite the spec for this.
What ships
Built on the client's direct request. Colours read from custom properties with the Figma value as a fallback, so any ancestor rule wins by inheritance: .tvs-platter for every Platter button on the page, the section's BEM base for every instance, and #<Section ID> for one placement.
Why
Hover is derived from whatever background it is given, so overriding one value does not leave a stale hover behind. Verified in Chrome at all three scopes.

Button hover matches Figma except on the filled palettes

Differs

Spec & design
Figma 7078:62 specifies hover and disabled. Focus is not covered there.
What ships
Outline and link hovers are fixed tokens and land on Figma exactly. The filled default lands on #012B5B against Figma's #012169 (dE 0.038 — both dark navy). Focus-visible is ours.
Why
Figma's hover colours are not one formula — #0458AD→#012169 is a 63% mix toward black but #F2F2F2→#D9D9D6 is 92%, outline-light lightens, and both link hovers change hue outright. The derived hover is the deliberate cost of hover following a page-level override automatically.

Alt text is per crop, swapped in JavaScript

Differs

Spec & design
“Admin can upload alt text for each image”, with separate desktop and mobile crops.
What ships
An optional imageAltMobile falling back to imageAlt. One <picture> has one <img> and therefore one alt, so the crop on screen is tracked in JS and the alt swapped reactively.
Why
Two <img> elements would satisfy the letter of the spec at the cost of a double fetch on an LCP image. The fallback also covers the case where no mobile crop was uploaded, since phones are then served the desktop image.

Differences 10

The quote ships in Lato where Figma specifies Fields

Differs

Spec & design
The quote is styled typography/family/primary, which resolves to Fields.
What ships
Lato, which the design system names family/secondary and already uses for the name and credential.
Why
Visible consequence: Lato sets narrower than Fields, so the design's four-line quote sets in three at the same 36px and the card is correspondingly shorter than the 615px frame. Nothing is clipped. Unlike the other Fields sections this one does not fall back to a serif at all.

Portrait and Circle are two layouts, not one layout with a different crop

Differs

Spec & design
“Admin can toggle Portrait vs Circle. This also changes the background card display.”
What ships
One content type with an assetType toggle and two genuinely different layout branches. Only one is ever in the DOM.
Why
Portrait puts the media in a card of its own with name and credential under the quote, and the quote card bleeds off the right edge. Circle is a single inset card with a round avatar in its own left column, name and credential centred beneath it, and a 3px rule between that column and the quote. QA needs both described; the FSD describes neither.

Mobile changes the media's aspect ratio

Differs

Spec & design
The FSD says only that the media “stacks above the quote card, full-width, on mobile”, which reads as the same crop.
What ships
Figma 18227:294436 makes the Portrait media 343 × 193 — 16:9 landscape on phones, against 314 × 535 portrait on desktop. Built to the frames, from a single uploaded image, object-cover-cropped to each ratio.
Why
One asset per instance is what the FSD review already told the client this module takes. The cost is real: a 9:16 source shows a band cut from its middle on phones, so the field description asks for the subject centred with room above and below the face. Circle is 1:1 at both breakpoints and is unaffected.

The Portrait quote card bleeds off the right edge

Differs

Spec & design
Not in the FSD at all, and not something an implementer would infer from it.
What ships
The Figma frame has padding-right: 0 and square right corners, so the gradient runs to the viewport edge. Built faithfully at every width.
Why
The quote's own measure is capped at the design's 902px so a wide monitor does not stretch it to an unreadable line length — the card grows, the text does not.

Above 1512 the Portrait media aligns to a centred container

Differs

Spec & design
Figma draws one desktop frame, at 1512, so anything wider is undefined.
What ships
The left inset tracks where a centred 1416px container would start. A no-op at 1512 exactly, and clamped to 48px on anything narrower.
Why
A flat 48px inset would leave the media pinned to the far left of a 2560px monitor while the card bleeds right, which reads as detached rather than full-bleed. The right edge still bleeds, because that is the design.

Background colour is a checked list, with two overrides behind it

Differs

Spec & design
“Admin can choose color background. Solid. Gradient.” No colours and no constraint.
What ships
An enum of four — the Figma gradient, flat light blue, flat pale blue and white — all keeping #012169 text at or above 6.8:1. Behind it, an optional colour picker and an optional raw CSS gradient, added at TVS's request. Precedence is gradient, then colour, then preset.
Why
A merchandiser who touches nothing cannot produce an unreadable card. Neither override is contrast-checked and the quote stays navy, which is the known cost of the freedom.

The video modal is a native dialog, and it opens playing with sound

Differs

Spec & design
One sentence: “Clicking the video opens a modal with native video controller UI included.”
What ships
A native <dialog> opened with showModal(), which supplies the focus trap, Escape-to-close and focus return. Backdrop click closes it and the video is paused on close. Opening plays immediately, with sound. The trigger is the whole media, not just the play circle.
Why
The FSD's Video Modal row says “clicking the video opens a modal” while its User requirement says “tap the play button overlay”; one full-bleed button satisfies both, and on a phone it is the difference between a 48px target and the whole card. Unmuted is only permitted because play() runs inside the click's user gesture; where a browser refuses, the rejection is swallowed and the native controls are the fallback.

Video is ignored on the Circle asset type

Differs

Spec & design
The FSD does not say which asset types accept a video.
What ships
The play button and dialog render only for portrait. A video set on Circle is ignored.
Why
The play button is positioned against the vertical media card and a 1:1 avatar has nowhere to put it. Stated in the field description so a merchandiser is not left guessing.

The expert's name is the section's accessible name

Differs

Spec & design
There is no heading field anywhere in the FSD or the frames.
What ships
The section root is labelled by the expert's name, and the quote is marked up as a blockquote.
Why
Rather than invent a heading the design does not show. This satisfies the landmark requirement but not the heading-order one the Global SEO FSD asks for — the module contributes nothing to the page outline.

Pressing play fires ItemClick, not a video event

Differs

Spec & design
Click tracking via the standard raiseEvent handler; nothing about video.
What ships
The same ItemClick the CTA fires, with action: 'video-play' in the payload to tell them apart.
Why
There is no video-play event name anywhere in the ten downloaded TVS templates. If TVS have a real one it should replace this.

Findings 4

Video has no caption track, and no field for one

FindingBlocked — needs a decision from TVS

No captions or subtitle track is specified anywhere in the FSD and there is no field to hold one. A talking-head video without captions fails WCAG 1.2.2, and accessibility is a delivery requirement on this project — so this is a gap rather than a deferral. It needs either a <track> field or a decision that TVS burn captions into the file.

A valid .mp4 can still be unplayable, and nothing checks

FindingShipped with a documented risk

“Admin can upload .mp4” treats mp4 as one thing; it is a container. The demo asset supplied for this module was AV1, 2160 × 3840, 17.9MB, with the moov atom after mdat — a valid .mp4 that will not play usably on a storefront, because AV1 needs recent hardware to decode on Safari and iOS and a trailing moov means nothing starts until all 17.9MB has arrived. Re-encoded for the preview to H.264 High, 1080 × 1920, faststart, 4.37MB. The requirement is written into the field description in plain language but nothing enforces it — the field is a URL string, so a merchandiser can paste an AV1 file and it fails silently at play time.

Video hosting is answered by observation, not by the FSD

FindingField is still a plain URL string

The FSD does not say where the .mp4 lives. TVS have already answered it in practice: everything on the QA German Creatine page is served from Amplience, either as a rendition URL (cdn.media.amplience.net/v/vitaminshoppe/…/mp4_720p) or as the resolved static file. No third-party player appears in any of the ten downloaded templates. Asking a merchandiser for a rendition URL rather than a raw file would remove the codec, faststart and file-size problems in one move — but mp4_480p and mp4_1080p return 404 for that asset, so the rendition set is configured per asset rather than being universal.

The video size cap is a number we chose

FindingAwaiting a number from TVS

“Max size: TBD” is still TBD. The schema description asks for under 10MB, which is a number we picked to have something actionable in the CMS, not one TVS gave us. Nothing enforces it.

08 — sticky-nav-bar

sticky-nav-bar

Template 1

reference page and design comparison

Differences 7

The “disable global sticky header” checkbox is not built

Not built

Spec & design
The Admin requirement's third row asks for a checkbox that unpins TVS's global site header, so that only this nav is sticky on scroll.
What ships
Not built. There is no such field in the CMS.
Why
Descoped on the client's instruction 2026-08-11. The unchecked behaviour is unaffected and is the one that matters — the nav parks below whatever TVS has at the top of the viewport, measured at run time. Nothing this section ships reaches outside its own markup.

An anchor row is a general link field, not a section-ID picker

Differs

Spec & design
“Anchor target section (Use the mapping section ID).”
What ships
ctaLabel + ctaUrl per row; an on-page target is written as #<section id>.
Why
A superset of the spec — every value it describes is still expressible — and the field names match every other CTA in the library. The cost is that a merchandiser can enter a link the active-state indicator cannot track: only # links are eligible for the underline, and anything else navigates but never highlights. That is stated in the field's own description.

The 2–4 link count is enforced by the form

Differs

Spec & design
“Anchor Link (Required, x2-4).”
What ships
minItems: 2, maxItems: 4, enforced by Amplience rather than by the template — the form refuses to save one link or five.
Why
Worth testing in the CMS rather than on the page: the markup never has to handle those cases because they cannot be authored.

The bar is position: fixed, with a placeholder holding its height

Differs

Spec & design
“It becomes sticky after the user scrolls past it.”
What ships
position: fixed, and an outer element holds the vacated height open.
Why
A sticky element only sticks within its own containing block, and Amplience wraps every content item in a <tvs-cms-component> whose height is exactly this section's height — so position: sticky would unstick on the same frame it started sticking. The placeholder is what stops the page below jumping up at the moment it detaches. Verified in Chrome at 1280×760 and 375×700.

The parking offset is measured from TVS's header at run time

Differs

Spec & design
The FSD says the nav attaches underneath the global site header; it names no height.
What ships
The section resolves TVS's header, reads its live bottom edge, and parks there — but only where that element actually computes to fixed or sticky, is against the top edge, and is shorter than 40% of the viewport. Where neither known selector matches, it asks what is at the top edge instead. A page rule declaring --plx-sticky-nav--offset overrides it outright.
Why
A hard-coded number is wrong in four ordinary situations: TVS's header does not pin until their own scroll handler fires, the mobile band height is not obliged to match desktop, a promo strip above the header changes the total, and the older production header does not pin at all. QA note: TVS pin their header from their own scroll handler, so a programmatic scroll never fires it — the header only appears under a real wheel gesture.

The active indicator is a pseudo-element, and hover is ours

Differs

Spec & design
Figma draws the active indicator as a 4px bottom border on the link, #41b6e6 — the lighter blue, not brand blue. Hover and focus are not specified for the links.
What ships
Pixel-wise the same line, drawn as a pseudo-element. Hover draws it at half opacity; the active link stays at full. Focus-visible takes the navy ink.
Why
A real border-bottom sits inside the box, so on a 52px link with centred text the type would jump 2px upwards on whichever link happened to be active. Differences 10

The total sums the price shown on each row, not the compare-at price

Differs

Spec & design
Product Card says “Price (only final price price)”, so each row shows the price actually charged. Add to cart button says “Total price is dynamically calculated by adding SKU's compare at price”.
What ships
The total adds up the prices on screen.
Why
The design's own arithmetic points the other way: three rows at $25.99 sit under a total of $77.97, which is the sum of the prices shown. Taken literally, whenever anything in the bundle is on sale the module prints three prices that visibly do not add up to the total directly beneath them — a pricing claim a shopper can check in their head and find wrong. If the client confirms the compare-at basis it is a one-line change.

The bundle photograph is a field the spec does not list

Differs

Spec & design
The Admin requirement table lists Label, Heading, SKU Selection, Product Card, Add to cart button and Promotion Summary — no image field. Both frames draw a large lifestyle photograph.
What ships
image and imageAlt required, imageMobile and imageAltMobile optional.
Why
The section cannot render its own design without one, and the photograph is clearly authored rather than pulled from a SKU. Open question: confirm the field set, and whether the mobile crop is a separate asset or the desktop one re-framed.

The label chip is not uppercased

Differs

Spec & design
The design draws the chip in sentence case. The shared eyebrow field is described as uppercasing itself, and every other section that uses it does.
What ships
The chip renders in sentence case, matching the frames — the uppercase treatment is not applied here.
Why
The uppercasing was always markup rather than a property of the field, so nothing about the shared field changed. Its description in this section says which behaviour applies.

The Add to Cart button wears the shared Platter button

Differs

Spec & design
Figma draws this button 55px tall on desktop and 41px on mobile, with 16px text on both and no letter-spacing.
What ships
The shared button, which resolves to about 50px and 43px with 0.04em tracking. Only the mobile font size is overridden, so the label matches the design's 16px.
Why
The remaining few pixels of height are the price of one button across the whole component set. A per-section box would mean loose button fields, which the project forbids.

The photograph takes its height from the text column

Differs

Spec & design
The design's causality is the reverse — the desktop frame's column heights sum exactly to 654, so the text column is height-matched to the photograph.
What ships
On the two-column desktop layout the photograph spans both grid rows and stretches to whatever the text column comes to. It is object-cover, so it crops rather than distorts.
Why
Turning the promotion summary off removes about 105px from that column and the photograph crops that much harder. A height floor was considered and deliberately not added: every candidate value is arbitrary, and the drawn 654px would make the photograph taller than it is wide at the narrower desktop widths.

The product thumbnail is part of the row's link

Differs

Spec & design
The design draws no link on the thumbnail, leaving the product name's text line — 24px tall, rows 12px apart — as the only target in the row.
What ships
The thumbnail is wrapped in an anchor to the same destination, hidden from the tab order and from screen readers, so the row still contributes exactly one link.
Why
On a touch screen a shopper taps the product image and gets nothing. This gives the row a 92px target in place of a 27px line of text.

The header is hoisted above the photograph on mobile

Differs

Spec & design
Desktop is the photograph beside a column holding the header, the products, the button and the summary. Mobile puts the header first, then the photograph, then everything else.
What ships
A grid whose DOM order is header, photograph, content — the mobile order — with explicit placement putting the header back into the right-hand column on desktop.
Why
The two frames are not a reflow of one source order, and a flex container that only flips direction cannot produce both. No other Platter section reorders a child between breakpoints.

An unresolvable SKU hides the bundle, as an out-of-stock one does

Differs

Spec & design
“If any included SKU is out of stock, the entire section is hidden.” The spec does not say what happens when a SKU no longer exists.
What ships
Any SKU that fails to resolve hides the whole module. The failure is logged to the console.
Why
A bundle is priced and sold as a set, so a missing product would mean advertising a total for goods the shopper cannot buy. This deliberately differs from the product modules, which drop the dead card and keep going.

Product names are clamped to two lines

Differs

Spec & design
The mobile frame specifies a two-line clamp; the desktop frame does not, though both draw the name over two lines.
What ships
Clamped to two lines with an ellipsis at both breakpoints.
Why
The sample copy happens to fill exactly two lines at the drawn width. A longer name would push the row past the photograph beside it and open a gap under the summary.

The mobile struck price matches the desktop treatment

Differs

Spec & design
On the mobile button the struck was-price is drawn at 18px and the payable price at 16px; on desktop both are 16px.
What ships
Both render at the shared button size on both breakpoints.
Why
The two frames treat the same pair differently, and emphasising the price the customer is not charged is the less usual of the two, so we followed the desktop treatment. Open question: confirm, or tell us the mobile emphasis is intended.

Findings 3

Nothing checks that the authored discount matches a real promotion

FindingNeeds a process from TVS to keep the two in step

The discount is authored: the merchandiser types a promotion name and either a percentage or a dollar amount, and the section computes the subtotal from it. Nothing verifies that a matching promotion exists in TVS's promotion engine, so a bundle can advertise a saving the cart will not apply. TVS-Common exposes no promotion API to us, so we cannot check it from the front end.

The summary rows may be meant as two columns

FindingNeeds design confirmation

Every visible row of the promotion summary carries a hidden second text node anchored to the row's right edge, and each row is drawn as a flex container with a gap. The visible state collapses to one left-aligned string, which is what we built, but the reserved structure is plainly a label-on-the-left, amount-on-the-right row. That reading also explains the promotion row, which as drawn reads as a highlighter stroke across the column.

The info glyph in the label chip does nothing

FindingNeeds client confirmation

The label chip carries a 16px info-circle after its text on both frames. Nothing in the spec mentions it and no tooltip, popover or target is drawn anywhere in the file. It renders as decoration, hidden from assistive technology. If it is meant to disclose the promotion's terms it needs both the copy and a disclosure pattern, neither of which exists.

Differences 4

The tile count is a 2–8 range, not “4 or 6”

Differs

Spec & design
The Goal Tile row header reads “Required, 4 or 6”, while the Responsive cell in the same row gives the example “5 tiles render larger than 6 tiles at the same viewport” — which only makes sense if 5 is permitted. Figma provides 4-tile and 6-tile frames only.
What ships
A 2–8 range.
Why
The same cell states the tile size “is not fixed — it scales based on the number of tiles added”, which is a formula rather than an enumeration. Both ends of the range are legal and neither looks like the mock: two tiles are 680px wide with an 850px-tall photo each, a module about 1,000px deep holding two pictures. 4 to 6 is the range the design actually supports. The bounds are ours — neither is in the spec.

The whole tile is the link, not just the arrow

Differs

Spec & design
“User can click a goal tile to navigate…”. The arrow carries no interaction of its own in Figma.
What ships
A single anchor per tile wrapping image, label and arrow. The arrow is decorative.
Why
The hit area is the whole card and screen readers announce one link rather than an image link plus a nameless button.

Tiles have no separate mobile crop

Differs

Spec & design
The shared vocabulary offers a separate phone crop; the asset requirement gives one 4:5 ratio.
What ships
One image and one alt per tile.
Why
The tile is 4:5 at every breakpoint — only its width changes — so a second crop would be the same picture at a different size, which is a CDN concern rather than a merchandiser one. It would also put four extra fields in a repeater row that already has three.

Hover and focus on the arrow are ours, and the first-tile scrim is not built

Differs

Spec & design
Every arrow instance is placed at State=Default, so hover and focus are not drawn for it here. Separately, the first tile of each of the four frames carries a full-bleed rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.2) scrim over its photo, matching the image's corner radius; tiles 2–n have none.
What ships
Hover moves the circle to the existing brand blue, guarded so a tap does not leave it stuck lit. Focus is ours. The scrim is not built.
Why
The scrim appears on the first tile only, which fits either a hover or pressed treatment shown in place, or something belonging to that particular placeholder photo. Open question — if it is an interaction state we need it as a variant rather than a static overlay, and we would rather build it than guess at it.
